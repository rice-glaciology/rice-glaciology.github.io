The National Academies' [Gulf Research Program](https://www.nationalacademies.org/programs/GULF-GEPS-21-P-370) has awarded our group funding to lead *Shifting Land, Rising Seas: Forecasting How Human and Climate Drivers Shape Gulf Coast Sea-Level Futures* — one of four projects in a [$7.3 million round](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/gulf-research-program-awards-over-7-million-to-advance-scientific-understanding-of-sea-level-variation-and-rise-in-the-gulf-region) advancing the science of sea-level variation and rise in the Gulf region.

The project tackles major uncertainties in Gulf Coast sea-level-rise projections by integrating vertical land motion, hydroclimate variability, and community vulnerability into a single forecasting framework. We will combine geodetic observations with poromechanical models of subsidence and glacial isostatic adjustment, and climate-model large ensembles that capture short-term variability such as El Niño–Southern Oscillation and decadal atmospheric patterns. These physical projections will be paired with a dynamic vulnerability index built from data on infrastructure abandonment, population migration, and housing trends.

The work will deliver high-resolution subsidence datasets, improved probabilistic sea-level projections that account for both geophysical and human drivers, integration of the results into the Framework for Assessing Changes to Sea-Level (FACTS), and interactive mapping and visualization tools to support adaptation planning along the coast.
